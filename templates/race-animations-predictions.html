🏇 Race Animations & Predictions

Every runner jumps from its barrier, crosses to the lane its speed-map role calls for, and holds that settled position until the corner — then the blended prediction score decides the run home, in the manner the tempo of the race calls for. Where the race has been run, the actual result is scored against the prediction underneath, so the picture can be checked against what really happened.

🔎 Reverse the race
The race is run and something else won. This asks the question backwards: what would the weighting have had to be for that horse to have rated top? It solves for the nearest split to the one you are on that gets there — or tells you straight out that no weighting would have found it, which is its own answer.

Composite score breakdown

Each input is scored by where the runner sits in this field, so one freak number cannot distort everybody else's. Tap a row to pick that runner out on the track — and on a race that has already been run, to solve for what would have rated it top. An italic value marked * means the runner had no data for that input and was given the field average rather than a zero. Only the inputs actually carrying weight get a column.

Your weighting Move a slider and the table re-scores straight away. The animation is rebuilt to the new order too, so hit Run the race to watch it. Your split is remembered on this browser and carries over to the next race you pick.
Does this weighting actually work?
Score it against every past race we hold a result for, or let the history pick a weighting of its own. The tuner trains on older races and reports its score on later ones it never saw — an in-sample number would look wonderful and mean nothing. Where the misses point reverses every race this weighting got wrong and reports where those answers cluster: it is how a wet-track or a leaders' bias shows up as a number rather than a hunch.
